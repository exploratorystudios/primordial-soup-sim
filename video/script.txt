Four billion years ago, there was no life on Earth. Only water, rock… and chemistry.

[0:08]
This is a simulation of how that changed. We’re looking at a patch of ancient seafloor less than a millimetre wide, where hydrothermal vents pump out heat and raw chemicals.

[0:24]
Everything here is drawn at its true size. Zoom in tens of thousands of times, and those specks become molecules: nucleotides, each a phosphate, a sugar and a base, about a nanometre across. On warm mineral surfaces, they link into chains. This is RNA, the molecule that may have started it all.

[0:46]
Some chains fold into shapes that do something remarkable: they copy themselves, letter by letter, from the nucleotides around them. The copies aren’t perfect, so the best copiers win. Meanwhile, fatty molecules gather into bubbles that trap these replicators inside. A protocell: heredity, wrapped in a membrane.

[1:08]
When a protocell lineage keeps growing, dividing and copying its genes, it becomes something new: a living cell, a few micrometres across, with ribosomes building proteins, and a chromosome carrying its genes.

[1:26]
Now, time speeds up. Every division copies the genome with occasional mistakes, and natural selection does the rest. Lineages split into species. Some learn to harvest sunlight, flooding the ocean with oxygen: a poison to the old world, and fuel for the new. Others learn to eat each other.

[1:52]
Then, a lucky accident. A cell swallows an oxygen-breathing bacterium… and doesn’t digest it. It becomes a mitochondrion, a power plant inside the cell. This is a eukaryote: bigger, with a nucleus, and the ancestor of every plant and animal.

[2:12]
Some cells stop drifting apart after they divide. Stuck together, they share food and protection, and something new appears: a division of labour. Cells take on jobs: feeding, defending, moving. A few become a germline, budding off to start the next generation. Many cells… one organism.

[2:38]
Nothing in this story was scripted. Every step emerged from the one before it: chemistry, then heredity, then evolution. This is the tree of life that grew in this one world. Start a new one, and history unfolds differently every time.
